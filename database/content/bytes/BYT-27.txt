You know that feeling of being mentally stuck? One moment you’re paralyzed by a decision, the next you’re rushing through your to-do list, and then suddenly you have zero motivation for any of it. It turns out, ancient yogic wisdom has names for these mental obstacles that get in our way.

Here are three common mental traps and how to start freeing yourself from them.

**1. Overcome Decision Paralysis (Samsaya)**
*Samsaya* is that state of indecision where you’re stuck on the fence, weighing endless pros and cons. This happens when your mind is too cluttered, like an ocean in a storm. Trying to make a clear choice in this state is like trying to see the ocean floor through turbulent water—it’s impossible. You can only skim the surface. The solution isn't to think harder, but to go deeper. By creating moments of quiet, you allow the "mud" in your mind to settle, revealing the clarity of your own inner wisdom, or gut feeling. Your heart already knows the direction; you just need to quiet the noise to hear its signal.

**2. Escape the 'Busy' Trap (Pramada)**
*Pramada* is the carelessness, haste, and indifference that comes from being disconnected from the present moment. It’s when you do things without putting your heart into them, leading to sloppy work and a feeling of just going through the motions. We often blame our environment for making us rush, but this state is an internal one. The antidote is to intentionally slow down and infuse even the smallest tasks with presence. By bringing your full attention to what's in front of you—whether it's washing dishes or writing an email—you reconnect with your inner source of focus and excellence. It transforms chores into opportunities for mindfulness.

**3. Reignite Your Inner Drive (Alasya)**
This isn't just physical laziness; *Alasya* is a deeper lethargy or sloth that arises when you’ve lost your zest for life. It’s the result of accumulated disappointment, frustration, and mental baggage weighing you down until you feel like you can't move forward. Think of it like a car that's broken down from a lack of maintenance. This isn't a character flaw—it's a sign that your system needs a recharge. Reconnecting with your purpose and clearing out mental impurities can feel like a lifeline, reigniting the hope and energy you need to get back in motion.

### Your Practice

Tonight, before you fall asleep, bring to mind one dilemma or challenge you're currently facing. Silently ask your intuition for clarity on it. Don't try to force an answer or analyze it. Simply plant the question in your mind, trust the process, and notice what insight or feeling is there for you in the morning.

### A Final Thought

These mental obstacles aren't personal failings. They are simply signals inviting you to slow down, clear the inner clutter, and reconnect with the quiet wisdom you already possess.
