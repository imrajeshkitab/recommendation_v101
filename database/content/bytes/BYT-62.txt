Think about the very first thought that grabbed your attention this morning. Was it a worry about your to-do list? The lingering feeling of being tired? That first thought often acts like a filter, coloring your entire day before you've even had your coffee.
 
 Your mind operates like a radar system, automatically scanning and locking onto whatever seems loudest be it a problem at work, an anxious feeling, or a distraction on your phone. This is your "autopilot" mode, the reason you can drive a familiar route and suddenly realize you don't remember the journey. But there's another part of you, an inner observer. Imagine standing on a riverbank, simply watching the flow of your thoughts and feelings go by without getting swept into the current. This observer is the key to shifting from being controlled by your mind chatter to consciously choosing where you place your focus. It's the part of you that notices when you're stuck in the same old worry loop.
 
 The next time you feel overwhelmed by mental noise, try this. For just one minute, picture yourself standing beside a gently flowing stream. As a thought or feeling arises, imagine placing it on a leaf and simply watch it float by. Don't judge it or try to change it. Just observe it as it drifts away. Another thought will come; place it on another leaf. The goal isn't to empty your mind, but to realize you don't have to be carried away by every thought.
 
 Your mind doesn't have to be a turbulent ocean. By creating these small moments of observation, you're building a stable shore to rest on, giving you the clarity to navigate whatever life sends your way.
