The emotional baggage from old arguments and past hurts can feel like a weight you carry everywhere. It quietly drains your energy and clouds your peace of mind. What if you could finally set that weight down? Here are a few practical ways to release those lingering feelings and find a sense of resolution.

***

### **Actively Rewire Your Resentment**

Instead of passively waiting for negative feelings about someone to fade, you can actively change the narrative in your mind. Think of it like tending to a garden. When you find a weed of resentment, you can consciously plant a flower of goodwill in its place. This practice involves visualizing the person who hurt you and intentionally sending them thoughts of peace and well-wishing. When you breathe out, imagine your affection reaching them; when you breathe in, imagine you're clearing away the negativity between you. It might feel unnatural at first, but it’s a powerful way to reclaim your own inner peace, rather than letting someone else's actions dictate your emotional state.

### **Create a Dialogue for Closure**

Sometimes, what you need most is a conversation you can't have in real life. The "empty chair" technique gives you a stage to do just that. Place two chairs facing each other, sit in one, and imagine the person (or even a part of yourself) in the other. Speak your mind freely—say everything you need to say without a filter. Then, switch chairs. Take on their role and respond from their perspective. This isn't about being right; it's about creating a space for empathy and understanding. By voicing both sides, you can often arrive at a sense of connection and release that you couldn't achieve by just stewing in your own thoughts.

### **Get It Out to Go Within**

Your mind can feel like a tangled mess of thoughts and emotions. Two simple practices can help you sort through it. First, use a journal as a pressure-release valve. Spend a few minutes each day writing or drawing whatever you're feeling, without judgment. Just let it flow onto the page. This act of externalizing your thoughts helps you see them more clearly. Second, practice self-acceptance by connecting with your inner core. Sit quietly, close your eyes, and feel the space in your heart that is naturally free from fear or resentment. Resting in this inner sanctuary, even for a moment, reminds you that beneath the surface-level turmoil, there is always a source of calm you can return to.

***

### **Try This Today**

Find a quiet spot and set a timer for three minutes. Close your eyes and bring your attention to your heart. Imagine a space deep inside you that is completely calm and accepting. Don’t try to fix or change any feelings you have; simply rest in this inner space of peace and let that feeling of acceptance spread through your body.

***

### **Your Takeaway**

Finding peace isn’t about erasing the past. It’s about refusing to let the past have power over your present.
