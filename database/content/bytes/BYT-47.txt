The lens through which you view reality might be more distorted than you think. Imagine your mind is like a deep ocean. When the water is perfectly still, you can see straight to the bottom with total clarity. But most of the time, it is full of waves and whirlpool our thoughts, biases, and emotional reactions that churn up the water and obscure the view. Ancient yogic psychology offers a powerful way to understand and calm this inner turbulence.
 
 ### Clear Sight vs. Mental Fog
 
 The core insight is learning to distinguish between two fundamental ways of seeing the world: clear perception and distorted perception.
 
 * **Clear Perception (Pramana)** is seeing things as they truly are. This happens when your mind is calm and centered. It's the kind of knowing that feels grounded and true, whether it comes from a deep gut feeling, logical reasoning, or learning from a trusted source.
 * **Distorted Perception (Viparyaya)** is much more common. This is "wrong knowledge," where your view is colored by stress, fear, past experiences, and ingrained beliefs. it is like wearing smudged, colored glasses, you're

 not seeing reality, but your *interpretation* of it. Think of a powerful circus elephant who remains tied to a small plastic chair, conditioned to believe it can't escape. It has lost a true perspective of its own strength. Much of our daily anxiety and poor decision-making comes from this kind of mental fog.
 
 ### Your Practice: Separate Fact from Story
 
 The next time you feel upset or frustrated by something, take 60 seconds to try this.
 
 First, identify the objective fact of the situation. For example: "A colleague disagreed with my idea in a meeting."
 
 Next, notice the story your mind immediately created about that fact. It might sound like: "They were trying to undermine me," or "My ideas are never good enough." That story is the potential distortion the *Viparyaya*. The goal is simply to see the space between the event and your interpretation.
 
 ### Your Takeaway
 
 You don't need a perfectly silent mind to live well, just a clearer one. By learning to spot the difference between reality and the stories you tell yourself, you can start to clean your own lens and navigate life with far more wisdom and peace.
