There's a reason why ignoring a problem feels like it's only getting bigger. It's because problems are like your shadow—the more you run from them, the more persistently they follow you. But what if you turned around and walked toward them instead?

***

**Face Your Weakness to Defeat It.**
Think of that one habit you wish you could break or that task you keep avoiding. The natural impulse is to hide it or remove the temptation entirely. But this often gives the problem more power, making it chase you. The alternative is a form of exposure therapy: put the challenge right in front of you. If it's a craving, put the snack on the counter and ask yourself, "Am I stronger than this?" By confronting the issue directly instead of avoiding it, you're not just resisting a habit; you're building the mental muscle to prove you are in control, which strengthens your resolve in all areas of life.

**Focus on the Next Step, Not the Finish Line.**
Setting big, ambitious goals is great, but staring at how far away the summit is can be incredibly discouraging. It makes the journey feel impossible. A more powerful approach is to define your destination, but then lower your gaze and focus entirely on taking the very next step perfectly. Progress isn't made by staring at the distant future; it's built by the small, consistent actions you take right now. Occasionally, you can look up to make sure you're still on the right path, but your energy is best spent on the movement happening under your feet.

***

### Your Practice This Week

Identify one small "shadow" you've been running from—a task you're procrastinating on, a conversation you're avoiding, or a temptation you're hiding from. Instead of avoiding it, bring it out into the open. Write the task on a sticky note and put it on your monitor, or place that tempting snack on the table. Simply face it for a few minutes and acknowledge its presence without giving in. The goal isn't immediate victory, but to practice facing it down.

***

Problems shrink when you walk toward them. Your strength is found not in avoidance, but in the courage to take that first step.
