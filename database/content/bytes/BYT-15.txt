There's a law of physics that explains why your relationships, just like your bedroom, tend to get messy over time if left unattended. This natural slide towards disorder is called entropy, and understanding it is the key to building connections that last.
 
 ### Your Relationship is a System
 
 Think of any relationship as a living system. Over time, small misunderstandings, unspoken frustrations, and minor conflicts pile up like clutter in a room. Without intentional effort, this system naturally moves towards chaos and instability. This is entropy in action. The common response is to constantly "clean up" with transactional inputs apologizing, buying gifts, or having long, draining talks. But this requires constant energy and suggests the relationship is based on tolerance, not true stability.
 
 ### The Zero-Input Solution
 
 The most stable and harmonious relationships don't require constant repairs. Instead, they run on a different kind of energy: love. When a connection is built on a foundation of genuine love and acceptance, the need for constant "inputs" disappears. You don't have to explain every action or constantly patch things up because there's a deep, unspoken understanding. Love isn't the act of fixing the system; it's the force that makes the system stable from the start.
 
 ### Practice
 
 Think of one important relationship in your life. For the next minute, instead of focusing on any problems or things you need to "fix," simply connect with the feeling of love and acceptance you have for that person. Let that feeling be the focus, without any agenda.
