There's a subtle but powerful difference between the phrases "life is give and take" and "life is take and give." One builds walls, while the other builds bridges. Which one are you using in your relationships?

**The Give-First Principle**
The key to building deep, lasting trust is to be the one who gives first. When you operate on a "take and give" model, you’re essentially waiting for the other person to prove themselves before you offer anything. This communicates a fundamental lack of trust and often leads to a stalemate where both sides are waiting for the other to make the first move. By choosing to "give and take," you intentionally make yourself vulnerable. This act of giving first—whether it's an apology, a compliment, or a helping hand—is a powerful signal. It breaks the cycle of suspicion and shows you trust the process enough to initiate change, without demanding a guarantee of reciprocation. This applies everywhere, from healing a family rift to improving your work culture.

**Your Practice**
Think of one relationship in your life—with a partner, colleague, or friend—that feels stuck. You might be waiting for them to apologize, to reach out, or to change their behavior. For the next 24 hours, stop waiting. Instead, find one small, unconditional thing you can *give*. It could be a word of appreciation, offering to help with a task, or simply listening without judgment. Do it without any expectation of getting something in return.

**A Final Thought**
True change doesn't start by demanding it from others. It begins the moment you decide to offer the first piece of the bridge.
